\section{Vowels}
    Traditionally the vowels were produced by placing the following 3 symbols in different relation to each other. $\cdot$, ㅡ, ㅣ , which each represented heaven, earth, human respectively~\cite{hunminjeongeum}. While the philosophy behind it may be fascinating, for efficiency's sake it is easier to classify vowels in 3 groups. horizontal vowels $\mathrm{v}_1$, vertical vowels $\mathrm{v}_2$ and their combos $\mathrm{v}_1 + \mathrm{v}_2$.
    
    For ease of use I will use the following convention:

    \begin{equation}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            \text{hangul letter}\\
            \mathrm{vowel}_\text{reference word}
        \end{pmatrix}
    \end{equation}

    \subsection{Horizontal Vowels $\mathrm{v}_1$}
        They are vowels that have ㅡ (earth) as basis and fit into $\mathrm{v}_1$.

         \begin{equation}
            \begin{pmatrix}
                \text{ㅡ}\\
                \mathrm{eww}_\mathrm{''\textbf{eww!}''}
            \end{pmatrix}
        \end{equation}
        \begin{equation}
            \begin{pmatrix}
                \text{ㅗ}\\
                \mathrm{o}_\mathrm{g\textbf{o}}
            \end{pmatrix}
            \dotlink
            \begin{pmatrix}
                \text{ㅛ}\\
                \mathrm{yo}_\mathrm{\textbf{yo}ga}
            \end{pmatrix}
        \end{equation}
        \begin{equation}
            \begin{pmatrix}
                \text{ㅜ}\\
                \mathrm{oo}_\mathrm{m\textbf{oo}n}
            \end{pmatrix}
            \dotlink
            \begin{pmatrix}
                \text{ㅠ}\\
                \mathrm{you}_\mathrm{\textbf{you}}
            \end{pmatrix}
        \end{equation}

\subsection{Compound vowels $\mathrm{v}_1 + \mathrm{v}_2$}
    They are vowels that are a combination of a horizontal vowel $\mathrm{v}_1$ and a vertical vowel $\mathrm{v}_2$.
    
    \begin{equation}
       \begin{pmatrix}
            \text{ㅢ}\\
